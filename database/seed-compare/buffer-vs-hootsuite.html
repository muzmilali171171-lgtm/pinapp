Buffer and Hootsuite get compared constantly, but for Pinterest specifically, the gap between them is unusually wide. One is built to be the simplest, cheapest way to queue up posts. The other is an enterprise social operations platform where Pinterest is one line item among many. Here's how they actually stack up if Pinterest is your priority.

Pricing (2026)

Buffer's free plan covers up to 3 connected channels with 10 scheduled posts each — genuinely usable for someone just getting started with Pinterest scheduling. Paid tiers are priced per channel: Essentials runs around $6/channel/month, Team around $12/channel/month, with volume discounts kicking in as you connect more channels. A single Pinterest account on Essentials costs about $6/month.

Hootsuite dropped its free plan some time ago and now starts at $99/user/month for its Standard tier (up to 10 social accounts), scaling to $199/user/month for Professional and $399/user/month for Advanced, with custom Enterprise pricing above that. Critically, Hootsuite prices per user — adding a second team member roughly doubles your bill at every tier.

For a single person managing one Pinterest account, Buffer costs roughly $6/month. The equivalent single seat on Hootsuite costs $99/month minimum — over 15 times more for a comparable individual use case.

Pros and Cons

B
Buffer — best for individuals and small teams. Fast, clean, and genuinely affordable, with a free plan that covers real use. The trade-off: Pinterest-specific features are minimal — no smart timing, no templates, no recycling.
H
Hootsuite — best for large, multi-department teams. Genuinely strong governance, approval workflows, and social listening across many platforms at once. The trade-off: Pinterest gets no special treatment, and the per-user pricing makes it expensive fast for anyone outside a large organization.

The Honest Take

Unless you're already running Hootsuite for enterprise-wide social management and adding Pinterest to an existing setup, the pricing math rarely favors it for Pinterest specifically — you'd be paying for nine platforms' worth of infrastructure to schedule pins on one. Buffer is the more sensible default for most solo Pinterest marketers and small teams. Neither tool, though, does anything to help you create the pins in the first place.

Verdict

For Pinterest pin scheduling specifically, Buffer wins decisively for individuals, freelancers, and small teams — it's a fraction of the cost with everything a focused Pinterest workflow actually needs. Hootsuite only makes sense if you're already deep in its ecosystem managing Pinterest alongside many other channels at enterprise scale. Whichever you pick, pair it with a dedicated pin-creation tool if you want the design and writing work handled for you too.
