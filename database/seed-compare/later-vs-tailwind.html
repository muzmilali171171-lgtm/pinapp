Later and Tailwind are two of the most recommended tools for Pinterest scheduling, but they're built around different priorities. Later is a multi-platform visual planner that happens to support Pinterest well. Tailwind is a Pinterest specialist that has leaned even further into that focus over the past year. Neither one creates pins from your website content automatically — that's a separate layer worth understanding before you commit to either.

Pricing (2026)

Later's paid plans start at $25/month for its Starter tier (one social set, one user), with a Growth tier in the $45–50/month range and a top Scale/Advanced tier that runs $80–110/month depending on billing cycle — pricing here has shifted a few times, so it's worth double-checking Later's own pricing page before you commit.

Tailwind restructured its pricing to be Pinterest-first: a genuine free tier exists, with Pro starting at $29.99/month ($17.99/month billed annually), Advanced around $54.99/month, and Max at $99.99/month. Tailwind's plans are priced per Pinterest account, so a second connected account roughly doubles the bill.

Straight comparison: Tailwind's entry paid tier and Later's entry tier land in a similar range, but Tailwind offers a genuinely usable free plan first — Later currently does not. If you're testing the waters before committing to a paid tool, that difference matters.

Pros and Cons

L
Later — best for multi-platform creators. Strong visual planning, a genuinely nice drag-and-drop calendar, and support for six-plus platforms from one dashboard. The trade-off: Pinterest is treated as one of many channels, not the priority, and there's no evergreen recycling or Pinterest-specific optimization built in.
T
Tailwind — best for Pinterest-first marketers. SmartSchedule, SmartLoop, and Communities are all genuinely built around how Pinterest's algorithm works, and it's an official Pinterest Marketing Partner with deeper API access. The trade-off: it only covers Pinterest and Instagram, so a multi-platform strategy needs a second tool alongside it.

What Neither Tool Does

Both Later and Tailwind assume you already have a design ready to schedule. Neither one reads your website, generates a pin image from a blog post, writes a title or description for you, or automatically matches new pins to the right board. That's a distinct layer of the workflow — turning existing content into pins in the first place — that sits upstream of both tools.

Verdict

If your content strategy spans several platforms and you want one visual calendar for all of them, Later is the more practical choice. If Pinterest is your primary channel and you want tools built specifically around how it ranks and distributes content, Tailwind is the stronger specialist — and its free tier makes it easy to try before paying anything. Either way, both still leave the actual pin-creation work in your hands; that's the gap a dedicated auto-pin tool like AutomatedPin is built to close.
